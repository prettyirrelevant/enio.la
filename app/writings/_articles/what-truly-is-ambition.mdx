# What truly is ambition?

_This is me rambling, putting disorganised thoughts into words. Read it as that._

There is an ongoing [discourse](https://x.com/ISWISPodcast/status/2123780399348511109) on social media about a woman who earns more than her husband-to-be and pays for almost all of the wedding.

It begs the question: how is ambition influenced by hustle culture and our general lack?

Most of our problems are responsibilities: black tax, health challenges, housing, and so on. If most adults were free of these responsibilities, what would we chase? Would we then understand the European attitude to work better?

The discourse also equates ambition with earning power. A professor who chases academia is fulfilled and content. But to a person who measures by earning power, is that ambition? Or is that person less? We have other examples, such as a lawyer who does pro bono work, or the person who runs a shelter for abused women. One would consider these altruistic. So how exactly is ambition measured? Who determines the parameters? Is it fair, or is it biased?

Now let's come back to the couple. The lady earns 7 figures a month (let's assume a million naira), and the man earns less than 500k. Both come from nothing, so hustle culture is here. She is a first daughter, so she carries responsibilities and something I call "ada behaviour" — traits that first daughters exhibit, a mix of good and bad. They have been together for over 8 years.

I don't want to give talking points on something I don't fully understand — the peculiarity of their problem. But I have questions. They have been together for over 8 years. Did they never talk about money in that time? Surely resentment is setting in. Still, they are grown adults, and they can communicate, even if ego (on both sides, I assume) makes it difficult. The wedding is their first major financially draining project, so this is expected. But it sets the precedent for the ones that follow.

Then there is hypergamy, in the traditional sense. In a third-world country, many people do not separate tradition from current economic realities, and the internet amplifies that view. But the internet is not real life. A couple should make decisions on their own terms and by their own realities, not by internet talking points.

So let's not talk about the marriage. Zoom out, and face the actual problem: who decides what ambition is?

> Does everyone need to be ambitious? I don't think so, at least not in the way the internet measures it. To me, ambition is a drive toward something you value. For some people that is wealth or a title. For others it is a craft, a cause, or raising their kids. If the drive makes money as a side effect, fantastic. But money is not the measure.
>
> There is one floor, though. Everyone needs enough drive to carry their own responsibilities. That is the line between content and complacent. In a third-world country, that floor is high. Just to cover black tax, rent, hospital bills, food, clothing, and transport, you need to work much harder — or reach a much higher level — than someone elsewhere. So a lot of what we call ambition here is the cost of reaching the floor.
